1.4 Research Objectives and Research Questions





1.4.1 Research Objectives



This aim was pursued through three objectives. 





Objective 1 was to review adaptive learning approaches and existing cybersecurity awareness training systems to identify relevant features, limitations and design requirements for university students. 





Objective 2 was to 

analyse

 a publicly available cybersecurity awareness dataset using Python-based techniques to identify knowledge gaps, 

behavioural

 patterns and learner profiles. 





Objective 3 was to develop and evaluate a 

personalised

 adaptive training framework and prototype based on the 

learner

 profiles and identified requirements.





1.4.2 Research Question





RQ1 asked what adaptive learning and cybersecurity awareness training features are relevant for university students, supporting Objective 1. 





RQ2 asked what awareness and 

behavioural

 patterns can be identified in the dataset, and how they can support learner profiling, supporting Objective 2. 





RQ3 asked how learner profiles can be translated into requirements for an adaptive training framework, bridging Objectives 2 and 3. 





RQ4 asked how suitable the resulting framework and prototype are in terms of usability, relevance and design quality, supporting the evaluative strand of Objective 3.

1.6 Dataset and Data Availability



This study was conducted using the empirical data from the Data Set Cyber Security Issues in University and College Students in India, where the data has been posted on Mendeley Data by NIT Srinagar, 

Ravesa

 (2024) under CC BY 4.0 license, which allows reuse with attribution. The data includes 615 non-empty rows of respondents, in a total of 63 named columns, that were gathered through an online questionnaire which encompasses personal information, skill information, cybersecurity activities, cybercrime awareness, and cybercrime case reports. There are missing values and inconsistencies in several variables, and age is reported as a banded variable (17 to 25 years), so the respondents should not be assumed as being uniformly aged 18 or older, and a cleaning procedure and Python secondary interpretation were necessary. These items were not presented as a validated instrument, like the Human Aspects of Information Security Questionnaire, but instead were used here as indicative measures only. The use of this existing openly licensed dataset meant there was no need for additional data collection and allowed Objective 2 to be met.

1.8 Dissertation Structure



This dissertation continues in the following way: The literature review of this study is presented in Chapter 2, focusing on adaptive learning, cyber security awareness and learner profiling and the conceptual framework. Chapter 3 details the Design Science Research methodology, including the data analysis approach, prototype development and evaluation plan. The findings will be presented in the subsequent chapters, along with the framework and prototype developed, their assessment, and reflections.
