**Research Artefact Type:** Formative Usability, Relevance, and Design Evaluation Protocol  
**Methodological Alignment:** Chapter 3 (Section 3.9 & 3.10), Objective 3 (Evaluative Strand), and Research Question 4 (RQ4)

## 1. Overview and Evaluation Objectives

### 1.1 Purpose
This guide outlines the protocol for conducting a small-scale, formative evaluation of the low-fidelity prototype (`Cybersecurity_Training_Prototype.html`). In accordance with Chapter 3, the evaluation investigates:
1. **Usability (RQ4):** Measuring ease of navigation, clarity of instructions, and interaction efficiency using the standardised System Usability Scale (SUS).
2. **Perceived Relevance (RQ4):** Assessing whether university students perceive the diagnostic recommendations and learning scenarios as pertinent to student life.
3. **Design Quality & Transparency (RQ4):** Evaluating whether learners understand the deterministic adaptation rules and why specific modules were prioritised.

### 1.2 Evaluation Principles
* **Formative Targets (Not Statistical Proof):** The evaluation targets predefined formative benchmarks (mean $\text{SUS} \ge 68$, task independence $\ge 80\%$, median relevance $\ge 4.0/5.0$) rather than formal statistical hypothesis testing.
* **Neutral Facilitation:** The facilitator must observe without prompting or leading participants.
* **Ethics and Anonymity:** Participants must be adult volunteers ($\ge 18$ years) who have signed an independent participant information and consent form. No participant identifying details are stored in the prototype or linked to evaluation records.

## 2. Participant Test Procedure

### 2.1 Persona Walkthrough Tasks
Participants are assigned a scripted persona (e.g. Persona A: *Undergraduate with risky password habits and high phishing uncertainty*, matching empirical Profile 2/3 traits) to execute the following seven sequential tasks:

1. **Task 1: Complete Initial Diagnostic**  
   Navigate through Screen 1 (Welcome) and answer all six questions on Screen 2 (Initial Assessment) in accordance with the assigned persona sheet.
2. **Task 2: Identify Top Recommendation**  
   Examine the generated Personalised Learning Pathway (Screen 3) and identify which module is ranked #1.
3. **Task 3: Articulate Reason for Recommendation**  
   Read the explanation on the top module card and state in your own words why this topic was prioritised for your persona.
4. **Task 4: Complete Training Activity & Practice Scenario**  
   Open the recommended module, read the educational principles, select an option for the scenario decision, and review the feedback.
5. **Task 5: Complete Follow-Up Reassessment**  
   Answer the follow-up reassessment question at the bottom of the module screen and submit the response.
6. **Task 6: Identify Pathway Adaptation**  
   Examine Screen 5 (Feedback & Adaptation) and articulate whether the learning pathway changed, which module is now recommended next, and why.
7. **Task 7: Restart Session**  
   Activate the global "Restart Session" control, confirm the reset dialogue, and verify that the application returns cleanly to Screen 1.

## 4. System Usability Scale (SUS) Administration and Scoring

### 4.1 Standard 10-Item Instrument
The System Usability Scale (Brooke, 1996) is administered immediately following the walkthrough. Items are scored on a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (*Strongly Disagree*) to 5 (*Strongly Agree*).

* **Item 1:** I think that I would like to use this training prototype frequently.
* **Item 2:** I found the training prototype unnecessarily complex.
* **Item 3:** I thought the training prototype was easy to use.
* **Item 4:** I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this prototype.
* **Item 5:** I found the various functions in this prototype were well integrated.
* **Item 6:** I thought there was too much inconsistency in this training prototype.
* **Item 7:** I would imagine that most students would learn to use this prototype very quickly.
* **Item 8:** I found the prototype very cumbersome to use.
* **Item 9:** I felt very confident using the training prototype.
* **Item 10:** I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this prototype.

### 4.2 Standardised Scoring Calculation
*Source: Brooke, J. (1996). SUS: A 'quick and dirty' usability scale. In P. W. Jordan et al. (Eds.), Usability Evaluation in Industry (pp. 189–194). Taylor & Francis.*

For each participant response vector $R = [r_1, r_2, \dots, r_{10}]$ where $r_i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$:
1. **Odd Items (1, 3, 5, 7, 9):** Contribution score $c_i = r_i - 1$
2. **Even Items (2, 4, 6, 8, 10):** Contribution score $c_i = 5 - r_i$
3. **Total SUS Score:**  
   $$\text{SUS Total} = \left( \sum_{i=1}^{10} c_i \right) \times 2.5$$
   *(Note: The result is a normalised composite score out of 100, not a percentage).*
4. **Formative Threshold:** A mean SUS score of $\ge 68.0$ represents "above average" industry usability (Bangor, Kortum, & Miller, 2008).

3. **Adaptation Transparency:** "It was clear to me how my answers adjusted the order of recommended modules."

## 6. Optional Expert-Review Checklist

*Intended for 1–2 independent cybersecurity education / human-factors specialists.*

| Evaluation Dimension | Expert Review Criteria | Compliance / Rating (Pass / Partial / Fail) | Expert Observations & Recommended Refinements |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Pedagogical Alignment** | Do the module topics reflect authoritative guidance (NCSC, NIST SP 800-50r1, CISA)? | | |
| **2. Deterministic Consistency** | Are the adaptation and tie-breaking rules logically sound, transparent, and consistent? | | |
| **3. Safety & Privacy** | Are all domains safely reserved (`example.com`, `.invalid`) and reporting channels realistic yet generic? | | |
| **4. Accessibility & UI Quality** | Does the interface support visible keyboard focus, clear contrast, responsive viewports, and non-colour feedback? | | |
| **5. Ethical Restraint** | Does the prototype avoid inappropriate claims of permanent mastery from a single diagnostic? | | |

## 7. Ethical Protocol and Data Handling

1. **Informed Consent:** Administered separately prior to prototype access.
2. **Participant Autonomy:** Right to withdraw at any point without penalty.
3. **Zero Persistent Storage:** Responses are held exclusively in volatile RAM during the active session.
4. **Data Management:** Evaluation logs are stored anonymously in password-protected university cloud storage and destroyed following dissertation examination.
